Magic Keyboard "Pairing Mode": How It Actually Works

July 2026 · Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

If you're hunting for the pairing button on your Magic Keyboard, here's the answer up front: there isn't one. Apple's Magic Keyboard, Magic Trackpad, and Magic Mouse don't have a pairing mode you switch on — which is exactly why pairing them with a new device can feel so confusing.

A quick note on names, because it trips up half the searches for this: the aluminium Bluetooth keyboard Apple sold until 2015 was called the Apple Wireless Keyboard. In October 2015 it became the Magic Keyboard, gaining a rechargeable battery and a charging port. People use "Apple keyboard", "Apple wireless keyboard", and "Magic Keyboard" interchangeably, but the two generations genuinely behave differently when you pair them — so this guide covers both.

Here's how discoverability really works on Apple's accessories, and how to fix the classic "my Apple keyboard won't show up in Bluetooth" problem.

The rule: discoverable when on, but not connected

A Magic Keyboard advertises itself to nearby devices whenever two things are true:

  • The power switch (on the back edge — green visible means on) is on, and
  • it is not currently connected to any device.

That second condition trips everyone up. A Magic Keyboard that's paired to your other Mac — even one sitting asleep in a drawer — stays loyal to it and never appears in the new Mac's Bluetooth list. It's not broken; it's just taken.

Making it discoverable, step by step

  1. Break the existing connection. On the device it's currently paired with: System Settings > Bluetooth > hover the keyboard > Forget This Device. (No access to that device? Turning its Bluetooth off also works.)
  2. Power-cycle the keyboard. Slide the switch off, wait a few seconds, slide it on. It now starts advertising.
  3. Connect from the new device. Mac: System Settings > Bluetooth → the keyboard appears under Nearby Devices → Connect. iPad/iPhone: Settings > Bluetooth → tap the keyboard.

The shortcut that always works: plug the keyboard into your Mac with its charging cable (USB-C or Lightning, depending on the model). macOS pairs it automatically in about two seconds — no discovery, no waiting, and it steals the pairing even from another device. Unplug and it keeps working wirelessly. The same trick works for the Magic Trackpad, and for the Magic Mouse via its underside port.

Still not showing up? Quick checklist

  • Charge it. A dead battery looks exactly like a pairing problem. Plug it in for a few minutes.
  • Confirm the switch is actually on. Green visible = on.
  • Check the other Macs in your house. The #1 culprit: it silently reconnected to a previously-paired device the moment you power-cycled it.
  • Toggle Bluetooth on the Mac (off, wait 10 seconds, on) and try again.
  • Use the cable. Seriously — it bypasses every discovery problem at once.

Older Apple Wireless Keyboards work differently

Everything above describes the modern Magic Keyboard. If yours is the pre-2015 Apple Wireless Keyboard — the aluminium one with the cylindrical battery tube along the back edge — the procedure is genuinely different, and this is the one Apple keyboard that comes closest to having a real pairing button.

  1. Press and hold the round power button on the right end of the battery tube.
  2. Watch the small green LED just above it. A blinking LED means the keyboard is discoverable and advertising itself.
  3. Pair from the other device while it's still blinking. Discovery isn't indefinite — it gives up after a few minutes and you simply press and hold again.

Two things to know about this model. It runs on AA batteries, so if the LED doesn't light at all, replace those before assuming anything is broken. And because it has no charging port, the cable shortcut below isn't available to you — Bluetooth pairing is the only route. That single difference is why so much advice about "just plug it in" is useless if you own the older keyboard.

The same generational split applies across the line: the original Magic Mouse and Magic Trackpad also took AA batteries and had no port, while their 2015-and-later replacements recharge over Lightning, and the most recent models over USB-C.

Magic Trackpad and Magic Mouse: same rule, different switches

There's no pairing mode on these either — powered on and unconnected is the whole trick. What changes is where the hardware lives:

  • Magic Trackpad: power switch on the back edge. Charging port on the back edge too, so it stays usable while plugged in.
  • Magic Mouse: power switch on the underside, and — famously — the charging port is on the underside as well. Pairing it by cable works perfectly, but the mouse lies on its back and can't be used until you unplug it.
  • Magic Keyboard with Touch ID: pairs identically, but Touch ID itself only functions on Apple silicon Macs. On an Intel Mac it will type normally and simply won't authenticate.

If a trackpad or mouse won't appear in Bluetooth, run the same checklist as the keyboard. The overwhelmingly common cause is identical: it's still connected to another Mac somewhere in the house.

Common questions

Does the Magic Keyboard have a pairing button?

No. Unlike most third-party Bluetooth keyboards, it has no dedicated pairing button and no pairing mode. It becomes discoverable automatically whenever it's powered on and not connected to another device. If it isn't appearing, it's almost always still paired to something else.

How do you make an older Apple Wireless Keyboard discoverable?

Press and hold the round power button on the right end of the battery tube until the small green LED blinks, then pair from the other device while it's still blinking. This model takes AA batteries and has no charging port, so cable pairing isn't an option.

Is an "Apple keyboard" the same thing as a Magic Keyboard?

Same product line, two names. Apple Wireless Keyboard until 2015; Magic Keyboard from 2015 onward, when it gained a rechargeable battery and a charging port. The older one uses AA batteries and a press-and-hold power button; the newer one uses a sliding switch and charges over Lightning or USB-C.

Why is my Apple keyboard not showing up in Bluetooth?

Nearly always because it's still paired and connected to another device, so it never advertises itself. Forget it there or turn that device's Bluetooth off, then power-cycle the keyboard. Check the battery too — a flat battery looks exactly like a pairing fault. On a modern Magic Keyboard, the charging cable bypasses the problem entirely.

How do you put a Magic Trackpad or Magic Mouse in pairing mode?

You don't — there isn't one. Power it on while it isn't connected to anything and it advertises itself. The trackpad's switch is on its back edge, the mouse's is underneath. Both pair instantly over a charging cable as well.

Can you pair an Apple keyboard with an iPad, Windows PC, or Android phone?

Yes. It's a standard Bluetooth keyboard, so it pairs from any device's normal Bluetooth screen — Settings > Bluetooth on iPad and iPhone, Settings > Remotes and Devices on Apple TV, the usual pairing panel on Windows and Android. Same requirement as always: powered on, and not connected somewhere else.
